Area of Care

Orthopedics & Spine Care

Orthopedic care may range from managing chronic joint pain and sports injuries to joint replacement and spine procedures. The appropriate approach depends on your diagnosis, mobility, overall health, goals, and a qualified orthopedic specialist’s assessment.

Heal Before Home supports international guests exploring vetted orthopedic providers in the Philippines while coordinating the non-clinical arrangements around their stay, from consultations and arrival planning to accommodation, transportation, mobility support, rehabilitation scheduling, and return-home logistics.

Explore

Orthopedic Care Options

01

Hip Replacement

Total or partial hip replacement may be considered for severe arthritis, joint degeneration, fractures, or other conditions causing significant pain and loss of mobility. Damaged portions of the hip joint may be replaced with prosthetic components following specialist assessment.

02

Knee Replacement

Total or partial knee replacement may be considered for advanced arthritis, joint damage, deformity, or persistent pain that significantly affects mobility and daily life.

03

Spine Surgery

Surgery may be considered for selected spinal conditions such as herniated discs, spinal stenosis, degenerative disease, instability, nerve compression, or certain spinal deformities. Procedures may include decompression, discectomy, fusion, or other specialist techniques.

04

Arthroscopy & Sports Injury Surgery

Minimally invasive arthroscopic procedures may be used to diagnose or address joint injuries, including ACL and other ligament injuries, meniscus tears, cartilage damage, and selected sports-related conditions affecting the knee, shoulder, hip, or other joints.

05

Shoulder Surgery

Procedures may include rotator cuff repair, labral repair, stabilization, treatment of impingement or arthritis, and partial or total shoulder replacement when appropriate.

06

Fracture & Trauma Surgery

Orthopedic trauma care may involve surgical repair and stabilization of complex fractures or injuries using plates, screws, rods, pins, or other fixation techniques, depending on the location and severity of the injury.

07

Hand, Wrist, Foot & Ankle Surgery

Specialized orthopedic procedures may address conditions such as carpal tunnel syndrome, tendon or ligament injuries, trigger finger, bunions, hammertoes, fractures, arthritis, instability, and other disorders affecting the hands, wrists, feet, or ankles.

08

Other Orthopedic Treatments

  • Joint-preserving and cartilage procedures
  • Tendon and nerve procedures
  • Joint injections
  • PRP and selected regenerative therapies where offered
  • Non-surgical fracture care and bracing
  • Bursitis and tendinopathy management
  • Pain-management interventions
  • Physical rehabilitation

Availability depends on the provider, clinical assessment, and individual treatment needs.

Before choosing an orthopedic specialist

Know What to Ask Before You Decide

Orthopedic procedures can significantly affect long-term mobility, independence, and quality of life. A thorough consultation should help you understand your diagnosis, whether surgery is recommended, available non-surgical alternatives, expected recovery, rehabilitation requirements, and realistic functional outcomes.

Clinical Expertise & Surgical Experience

  • Surgeon Credentials: Who will perform my procedure, and what are their qualifications, board certifications, and experience with this specific surgery?
  • Procedural Experience: How frequently does the surgeon perform this procedure?
  • Implant or Prosthesis Selection: For joint replacement, which implant or prosthesis is being recommended, and why is it considered appropriate for me?

Treatment Rationale & Alternatives

  • Treatment Rationale: Why is this particular procedure or surgical approach being recommended for my condition?
  • Non-Surgical Options: Are there conservative, rehabilitative, minimally invasive, or other appropriate alternatives I should understand before deciding on surgery?
  • Expected Outcomes: What level of pain relief, mobility, and functional improvement may be realistic based on my condition, age, overall health, and current level of function?

Risks, Hospital Stay & Recovery

  • Risks & Limitations: What are the potential risks, complications, contraindications, and limitations associated with the procedure?
  • Hospital & Recovery Timeline: How long should I expect to remain in hospital, and how much time should I plan to stay near the treating facility after discharge?
  • Travel Readiness: What clinical milestones will you use to determine when I am ready to fly or undertake long-distance travel?

Rehabilitation & Follow-Up

  • Rehabilitation Plan: What physical therapy, mobility support, or rehabilitation may be recommended following the procedure, and when is it expected to begin?
  • Follow-Up Planning: What follow-up appointments, imaging, or monitoring may be required after surgery?
  • Continuity at Home: What information, records, or rehabilitation instructions should be shared with my healthcare professionals after I return home?

Understanding Your Options Matters

A reputable orthopedic provider should be able to explain the recommended treatment, reasonable alternatives, potential risks and benefits, expected recovery, and rehabilitation requirements so you can make an informed decision about your care.

Recovery & aftercare

Recovery Is Part of the Treatment

Orthopedic recovery can range from several days to several months depending on the condition and procedure. Joint replacement, spine surgery, fracture repair, and ligament reconstruction may require structured rehabilitation and a gradual return to movement.

Your treating provider and rehabilitation team will determine your individual recovery plan. Coordinating accommodation, transportation, mobility support, physical therapy, and follow-up in advance can make the recovery period easier to manage.

Follow Your Provider’s Recovery Plan

Medication, wound care, mobility restrictions, use of walkers or other assistive devices, exercises, and rehabilitation should follow the instructions of your treating provider and rehabilitation team.

Prioritize Rehabilitation

Physical therapy can be an important part of recovery after many orthopedic procedures. Understand when rehabilitation begins, how frequently it is recommended, and how it will continue after you return home.

Plan for Mobility

Based on your treating provider’s guidance and anticipated recovery needs, Heal Before Home coordinates the practical support required for safe and comfortable mobility. This may include wheelchair-accessible transportation, step-free accommodation, mobility equipment, bathroom assistance, and companion or caregiver support during the early stages of recovery.

Plan Before You Fly Home

Before your return home, Heal Before Home coordinates with your treating provider to help ensure you understand your post-operative instructions, follow-up schedule, medications, rehabilitation needs, mobility precautions, and any travel considerations related to your recovery.

Planning your journey

Plan Around Mobility and Recovery

Orthopedic surgery is only one part of your recovery journey. Traveling for care may involve pre-operative diagnostics, mobility considerations, specialized accommodation, and rehabilitation appointments. Heal Before Home coordinates the non-clinical arrangements around your medical plan, including accommodation, transportation, scheduling, and itinerary planning, so the details are in place before you leave home and your recovery experience can feel more seamless and supported.

After Treatment

Once your procedure is complete, Heal Before Home helps coordinate a seamless and supportive recovery experience.
